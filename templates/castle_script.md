## Fact check (sources checked 2026-11-25)
- Early castles were earth-and-timber (motte-and-bailey); timber palisades are vulnerable to fire. Standard.
- Curtain walls commonly 2-3 m thick, many 6-12 m tall; "up to ten feet thick" and "taller than three men" are conservative.
- Moats kept siege ladders/towers from reaching the wall (and hindered undermining; not narrated).
- Portcullis: wooden (oak) grid often iron-shod. Standard.
- **Softened:** murder holes — popular "boiling oil" replaced with stones and scalding water; oil was expensive and rarely used ([Wikipedia: Murder hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_hole), [ARH Foundation](https://www.arhfoundation.org/castle-gatehouse-murder-holes-explained)).
- Arrow slits (arrow loops) splay wide inside, narrow outside. Standard.
- **Softened:** clockwise stair "sword arm" story is presented as a popular claim and called "probably a myth": surveys find many anticlockwise stairs (~30% cited) ([Triskele Heritage](https://triskeleheritage.triskelepublishing.com/mediaeval-mythbusting-blog-2-the-man-who-invented-the-spiral-staircase-myth/), [Medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2012/08/the-rise-of-the-anti-clockwise-newel-stair/), [Fake History Hunter](https://fakehistoryhunter.net/2023/10/09/medieval-staircases-were-not-built-going-clockwise-for-the-defenders-advantage)).
- Trebuchet stones typically ~45-100 kg (≈100-220 lb); "heavier than a man" / "≈ 200 LB" sits at the upper end of typical ([Wikipedia: Trebuchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebuchet)).
- Concentric castles: inner wall higher so both rings can shoot. Standard (Beaumaris, Krak).
- Krak des Chevaliers 1271: Baibars' army mined and took the outer ward; the garrison in the inner ward surrendered after a forged letter (attributed to the Count of Tripoli or the Hospitaller Master — sources differ, so the script says only "a forged letter") ([Wikipedia: Fall of Krak des Chevaliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Krak_des_Chevaliers), [French Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/crac-chevaliers/en/baybars-siege-1271)).
