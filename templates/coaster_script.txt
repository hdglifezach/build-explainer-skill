# Build a Roller Coaster - narration lines (one take per line). "|| s" = pause after.
If you build a wooden ramp and push a cart off the top, you've got a roller coaster. || 0.45
But friction eats its speed, and it stops halfway up the next hill.
So you hook the cart to a chain and drag it to the top of the first hill.
Gravity does the rest. That's the lift hill.
But what if the chain snaps halfway up?
So you bolt a saw-tooth strip along the track, and a hinged latch on the cart drops into every tooth.
If the cart starts rolling back, the latch catches it. That's the anti-rollback.
And that click-clack on the way up? That's it, working.
But over the next hill, the cart wants to keep flying, and lifts right off the rails.
So you add wheels under and beside the rail, and clamp it from three sides.
Those are the upstop and guide wheels.
Now you want to go upside down, so you bend the track into a circle.
But to clear the top, it needs so much speed that riders at the bottom feel about six times their weight.
So you stretch the loop into a teardrop. Wide at the bottom, tight at the top.
That's the clothoid loop.
But the cart comes back way too fast, and brake pads wear out and overheat.
So you run metal fins under the cart between strong magnets. They slow it without touching.
That's the magnetic brake. || 0.5
Now it's safe. || 0.2
Until you add a second train, and the first one stalls.
So you split the track into sections, and only one train is allowed in each.
That's the block system. || 0.5
Koney Island's first loop, in eighteen ninety-five, was a perfect circle.
Riders hurt their necks so often, plenty of people paid just to watch.
All that engineering, just to fix what happens || 0.0
